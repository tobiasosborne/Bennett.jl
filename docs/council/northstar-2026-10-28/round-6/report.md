## opus

Both round-5 blocking issues are resolved: codex's B_DEFINED_NONDETERMINISM and opus's B_RESIDUAL_NONDETERMINISM. §3.2 now fixes every choice LLVM leaves open after poison and undef are removed. Floating point is deterministic soft-float in both builds, with fmuladd split into fmul+fadd, fast-math stripped and one NaN rule, so NaN bits, fusion and intermediate rounding cannot differ even through bitcasts and branches. Heap addresses come from a shared shim at a fixed base. Executions that depend on stack addresses are classified through provenance and validated against the interpreter. Memory faults are defined by an object-bounds model, and atomics are sequentialised under a recorded assumption. Each of these is a legal refinement of LLVM semantics, applied to the bitcode both builds compile from, so the 'equals native, or is flagged' oracle now rests on a contract native execution can satisfy. I found no new false premise or load-bearing gap, so the blocking list is empty. What remains are precision fixes. §3.2 contradicts itself on zero-initialising region inputs. Global and function addresses come from the linker, not the allocator shim. NaN canonicalisation must not reach bit-preserving ops (fneg, fabs, copysign). The FP rule and layout rule should be normative spec text, not defined by bennett-arith and bennett-runtimes code. The excluded (undefined and layout-dependent) fractions need a ceiling in the acceptance tests. A no-fork shadow-stack rewrite of allocas would shrink the layout-dependent class almost to nothing.

Together with deleting the zero-initialisation contradiction in §3.2, that moves C6, C2 and C8 to 5.
